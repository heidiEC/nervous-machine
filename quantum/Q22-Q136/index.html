Two qubits, 22 coupling-hops apart, moving as one signal.

On IBM's ibm_fez, qubits Q22 and Q136 sit far apart in the coupling map — 22 hops, no direct gate path. Standard per-qubit telemetry treats them as independent, because the feed is per-qubit by construction. Nervous Machine discovered they aren't independent, validated the coupling across two events, and localized it to shared readout electronics.

01Claim

Q22 and Q136 are coupled by a shared physical signal path, not coincidence.

Cluster 1fa2d19a (Q22, Q136) shows lag-0 dominance ratio of 5.41 — their simultaneous correlation is 5.4× stronger than their one-step-lagged correlation. That signature is consistent with a shared readout amplifier, multiplexed line, or shared LO — not with one qubit's drift propagating to the other through any time-dependent process.

The coupling map distance — 22 hops, no two-qubit gate path — rules out the gate-induced explanations a reviewer would reach for first. Whatever connects these two qubits doesn't live in the qubit lattice.

02Evidence

Two independent events, three months apart, both peaking the same way.

The pipeline registered the cluster from telemetry alone in February. We then watched it through two unrelated event windows. Both events drove the within-pair correlation toward 1.0 simultaneously, consistent with the shared-path claim and inconsistent with a one-off coincidence.

03Data

Public IBM Quantum telemetry. Pulled daily. No privileged access.

Per-qubit calibration properties — T1, T2, readout error, single- and two-qubit gate errors — pulled daily from IBM Quantum Platform between 2026-02-12 and 2026-05-28 (106 days, 96% validation coverage). Q22 and Q136 are two of 156 qubits on ibm_fez (Heron r2). The pipeline used no coupling-map topology, no vendor-side cluster hints, and no non-public data of any kind.

The same data is available to anyone with an IBM Quantum account. We pulled and archived it; we did not produce it.

04Method

Rolling correlation, lag dominance, validated against an independent event.

Cluster discovery uses rolling-window pairwise correlation across the per-qubit telemetry matrix; pairs with persistent within-window |r| above a noise-floor null distribution are registered as candidate clusters. Confirmation uses a lag-correlation test: a real shared-signal-path coupling produces lag-0 dominance, because the physical path carries the disturbance to both qubits simultaneously. A cascading artifact — where one qubit's drift propagates to another through some time-dependent process — produces comparable lag-0 and lag-±1 amplitude. Q22 ↔ Q136 sits firmly in the first category.

Methodology is published in full in Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.20575885, including the certainty-update mechanism that drives edge convergence and the null-distribution construction. The production implementation — tuned for live telemetry, integrated with alerting, and deployed against private fleets — is what we license.

05Falsifiability

The claim is checkable, and we publicly retract when checks go against us.

Every claim on this page names a number, a window, and a test from a methodology that is published in full. Anyone working from the methods paper can construct an independent check; we expect to be checked, and we welcome it.

When prior findings have failed independent checks, we have withdrawn them publicly. The canonical example: an early "17 of 17 meas_map subsets" claim was retracted when Heron's measurement map turned out to be a single monolithic group, which made the subset framing vacuous. The retraction lives at /assurance alongside others. Most vendor artifacts never list a withdrawn claim. Ours does.

What standard telemetry contains, what NM derives

A structural gap, not a vendor mistake.

This isn't a critique of any specific vendor surface. Per-qubit telemetry feeds are per-qubit by construction — that's the schema, not an oversight. NM is built on top of those feeds to derive the relationships the schema doesn't carry.

What the standard feed contains

Q22 and Q136 each report their own T1, T2, readout error, and gate errors, independently, every calibration cycle. The coupling map lists topology edges between adjacent qubits. Neither field encodes any relationship between qubits that are 22 hops apart with no shared edge.

What NM derives from the same feed

Cluster 1fa2d19a registered Feb 18; lag-0 dominance ratio 5.41; coupling validated across an independent event May 12. Operator signal: don't co-route critical operations across Q22 and Q136 — their readout failures are correlated, even though their per-qubit metrics are not.
