Who pays now

The figures above size the problem, not what Nervous Machine can sell. The buyers are teams that hold telemetry and are accountable when an AI-proposed answer about a physical system is wrong. Ordered by how fast we expect them to close: a product team with a ship date buys faster than an operations committee.

Closes fastest · 3 in conversation

Space component manufacturers

Nervous Machine ships inside the component: star trackers, GNSS and radiation sensors, payload controllers. The part learns its own envelope on this bus, in this orbit, and reports when it can run and when its readings can be trusted. Self-hosted in the spacecraft; no ground loop required.

Why they pay: autonomy is a spec-sheet feature, and a part that flags its own bad reading is a lander that doesn’t tip. What they buy: an embedded licence per unit, after an integration pilot.

Shown on ibm_fez

Quantum compute and sensing

Hardware teams deciding which qubits and sensors to trust today, from the calibration telemetry they already keep. The same loop tells a quantum sensor when its reading is one to act on.

Why they pay: calibration time is machine time. What they buy: a pilot on their archive, then the advisor on the control stack.

Pilot · Orbital Clarity

Space operators and mission science

Payload advisor on each satellite; voxel certainty on the ground; the reading that closes a gap, shared in kilobytes. Space-weather and Earth-observation data providers sit in the same loop.

Why they pay: 38 of 49 satellites lost to a storm the model under-called. What they buy: the pilot, then per-asset advisors.

Shown on Marconi100

Supercomputer and data-center operations

Faults the alarms miss, across owners who don’t share a dashboard. Strong fit, slower close: many owners, procurement cycles, and the budget sits with facilities, not the model team.

Why they pay: 32 faults, 26 of 31 confirmed by the operations log.

Fleets and defense analysis

Robot and vehicle fleet operators take the policy advisor; defense analysts act on model output they didn’t build and need to know where it stops holding.

Why they pay: DIU moved 41–51% of prototypes to production; the rest stalled. Longest close: programs run on budget years.

The offer, and what is still to fill

A fixed-price pilot: the team brings an archive and one question. They get back a scored record of what is confirmed, a gap register, and a DEBRIEF their team can question. Qualifier for every conversation: who pays when the AI is wrong?

Still to fill: teams per segment, who holds the budget, and price. These come from the first priced conversations; this page does not estimate them.

Left out on purpose

Enterprise AI agents are not sized here: Nervous Machine scores measured outcomes, not language-only tasks, and agent platforms are likely to build their own checks. The rows above are physical systems, where an outside, independent scorekeeper fits.

Why this lands on measured systems

The value has to come from AI that delivers in specific places, where its answers can be checked against measurement: the robotics, space and defense rows above.

MIT NANDA (2025) names the cause of stalled pilots: systems “do not retain feedback, adapt to context, or improve over time.”

The loop is the claim

Each step has a close neighbor somewhere in the field. We found no published system that runs all four together.

01 · where the data lands

Earn certainty for each causal relation from local prediction error, in event time.

Nearest: adaptive conformal inference updates intervals online; HASDM corrects a density model with tracking data, centrally.

02 · over the link

Send only what stays uncertain, in kilobytes, instead of the raw telemetry.

Nearest: whylogs ships statistical profiles; Φ-sat discards cloudy images on board.

03 · big models

Large models re-hypothesize only where certainty stays low, and patch the prior.

Nearest: GR00T pairs a slow reasoning module with fast control inside one model; it does not escalate measured uncertainty.

04 · people

Brief the team on what is still unknown and what is needed to learn it.

Nearest: Labelbox sends uncertain items to labelers; Maven briefs analysts on data, not on model limits.

What Nervous Machine has shown so far

Gap 3 · published · quantum entry point

Qubit clusters on IBM’s ibm_fez

From 106 days of calibration telemetry on 156 qubits, found 17 clusters that move together, 15 confirmed by independent events. On the event window it scored AUC 0.718 where per-qubit anomaly detectors scored 0.37 and 0.34. It was the only method that reported where its own hypothesis did not fit.

doi:10.5281/zenodo.20575885
Gaps 2, 3 · published

Siloed operations on a supercomputer

On 2.5 years of multi-owner telemetry from CINECA’s Marconi100, the certainty map classed some relationships as drifting while their prediction error stayed healthy; their slopes roughly halved by 2022. Escalating one persistent residual added a missing driver and cut held-out error by 65%; two other proposed patches were rejected by the validation gate.

doi:10.5281/zenodo.20649152
Gap 2 · pre-registered

Hardware faults temperature alarms missed

On the same Marconi100 telemetry, the detectors flagged 32 hardware faults that temperature alarms never caught: 20 lost power-supply redundancies and 12 stalled fans whose siblings spun up to keep chips cool. Scored against CINECA’s own operations log, operators drained the affected node in 26 of the 31 cases that could be checked, against about 4 by chance.

Gaps 3, 4 · published

A 72-cycle world-model experiment

An LLM agent driven by an external prediction-error protocol found three structural errors in its own starting model. The same run caught the model describing its own behavior with high confidence when the record showed otherwise: self-reported AI uncertainty can’t be trusted without outside error.

doi:10.5281/zenodo.19463951
Gaps 2, 3 · reproduced

Mars surface radiation

On Curiosity’s dose record, the same solar driver earned opposite signs in different seasons, so Nervous Machine split the relationship by season; a single fitted model would average the two toward zero. A persistent dose deficit was registered as a gap and re-hypothesized as two dated solar-wind arrivals in 2024, which show in MAVEN plasma and magnetometer data on those dates. Two pre-registered reproductions matched 12 of 17 published numbers.

Gaps 1, 4 · demonstrated

Kilobyte links, payload advisor, lunar records and DEBRIEF

Learning over a Bluetooth Low Energy link on a small test robot; a satellite payload duty-cycle advisor; four lunar learning campaigns with stored records; and DEBRIEF briefing demos. No outside team has used DEBRIEF yet.

How to read these numbers

  • Forecast years differ by market (2027 to 2035), so the totals add annual figures from different years. Quote markets separately when precision matters.
  • Delivered shares come from surveys and mission records in each domain. They measure how often projects deliver, which is a stand-in for how much forecast value gets realized.
  • Quantum compute and sensing is shown but not added to the totals by default: its forecast is sourced, but no delivery survey exists for the domain yet, so its delivered share borrows the cross-industry AI ROI figures. Tick “add to totals” to include it.
  • Commercial space excludes launch and satellite manufacturing ($79B in 2035). Only the data and applications layer is counted.
  • Defense figures are FY2027 budget requests, not enacted appropriations.
  • Medium-confidence anchors were seen only in abstracts or secondary coverage. Check the primary source before putting them on a slide.
