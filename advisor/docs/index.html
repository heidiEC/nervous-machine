NM Advisor

A daemon on your payload computer that learns, on the box, what payloads do to its thermal, power, battery, and memory envelope — and answers one question over a local API: can this payload run, for how long, and when must it stop.

What it is — and deliberately isn't

nm-advisord sits beside your payload manager. Every cause-and-effect relationship it uses carries a certainty score earned from prediction error against your telemetry — it cannot be asserted, seeded high, or faked. Where evidence hasn't been earned, verdicts fall back to conservative per-class defaults and say so.

It is not a controller — it holds no actuators and commands nothing; your OS keeps every decision. It is not an LLM on the device — the learning law is a compiled ~330 KB artifact, sha256-verified before every start, with no cloud and no network in the learning loop. And it is not a dashboard — it queues typed signals when something deserves attention; a quiet board sends nothing.

The two guarantees. Advisory only, by construction: worst case is a wrong suggestion, which its own certainty math then erodes. Never fabricate: every verdict states its basis — earned (named edges, earned certainty, click-through receipts) or default (conservative envelope). Verdicts get more permissive as the advisor learns; the learning period is a visible payoff curve, not a black-box warmup.

Get the kit

The evaluation kit is a signed tarball, under 1 MB: nm-advisor-kit-0.1.2.tar.gz, delivered through your transfer feed at transfer.nervousmachine.com — the same append-only, tagged ledger everything else NM sends you lands in. With your consumer key:

export NM_TRANSFER_TOKEN=<your key>
python3 nm_transfer_fetch.py tags                # everything NM has sent you
python3 nm_transfer_fetch.py save advisor-kit    # download + sha256-verify the kit

The kit is self-contained — Python 3.9+ standard library only, nothing to pip install, no accounts needed for anything on this page.

The kernel artifact inside (nm-kernel/0.1.0 logit-v2) is a licensed binary; the loader verifies its hash and replays a golden trajectory before first use — a mismatch refuses to learn rather than mislearning. Targets: aarch64 Linux (CM5/Jetson), x86_64 Linux, Apple Silicon and Windows x64 (bench).

Quickstart — ten minutes on your laptop

Runs on macOS (Apple Silicon), Linux, and Windows x64 — plain python from any terminal, no WSL, no build step. The kernel self-verifies at load on every platform.

# from the kit root — no hardware, no install, nothing leaves your machine
python3 -m advisor.main --bench --speedup 30
# open http://127.0.0.1:8800  — the status page

python3 sdk/nm_advisor_client.py verdict imager
python3 sdk/nm_advisor_client.py quote --power-w 6 --duration-s 1800

Bench mode runs a synthetic satellite — every field labeled sim, because provenance rides with every number here — through the full lifecycle: calibrate on a quiet synthetic day, freeze the measurement scale, learn live, serve verdicts. docs/PLAY.md in the kit is the guided tour: start the imager and watch the RUN window count down; start the radio and watch a first contact earn instead of alarm; inject the cooling fault — the one thing that does alarm; stack payloads in eclipse until battery, not thermal, becomes the limiting factor.

Install on the CM5

sudo sh install.sh

The installer verifies the kernel artifacts against SHA256SUMS, creates a locked-down nm-advisor service user, copies code (read-only at runtime) to /opt/nm-advisor, seeds mutable state in /var/lib/nm-advisor, and installs the systemd unit. It does not start the daemon — calibration comes first, and the daemon will hold verdicts until it has a frozen scale. Then:

  1. Describe the board — /opt/nm-advisor/config.json: your device_id, thermal ceiling, power budget, SoC/memory floors, cadence.
  2. Describe the payloads — /var/lib/nm-advisor/payloads.json, one envelope block per payload (format below). Your scheduler can also register them live via POST /v0/payloads.
  3. Calibrate on real telemetry — at least 24 hours, idle plus at least one load burst (run your payloads by hand during the window): 
    cd /opt/nm-advisor && sudo -u nm-advisor python3 -m advisor.main \
  --device --calibrate 86400 \
  --results /var/lib/nm-advisor \
  --payloads /var/lib/nm-advisor/payloads.json
  4. Start and enable — sudo systemctl enable --now nm-advisord, then ask it something: python3 /opt/nm-advisor/sdk/nm_advisor_client.py verdict <payload_id>.

Calibration & gates — why it refuses

The gates are the product. Refusals are the system telling you the number you'd have gotten wasn't real:

gatebehavior
No learning before freezeCalibration finds the smallest measurement band per observable whose median error beats the bar; that band is frozen write-once, dated. Without it the daemon serves HOLD verdicts (basis: unavailable) and says so.
Explicit mode, always--bench (synthetic, declared sim) or --device (real readers) must be chosen — a real board never silently gets the simulator.
Kernel verify at every startsha256 against the manifest, then a golden-trajectory replay; failure refuses to learn.
Recalibration is a decisionRe-freezing a frozen observable is rejected; move the results dir aside — the old scale and its earned edges stay auditable. Newly sensed observables are the exception: they join additively as dated addenda, existing values untouched.

What the board can sense

The readers measure what the hardware provides and never fake the rest — fields are omitted, not invented, and every present field is source-flagged measured:

observablesourcebare CM5
soc_temp_c/sys/class/thermal✓ built-in
mem_available_mb/proc/meminfo✓ built-in
input_power_wINA-class power monitor on your carrierneeds instrumentation
pack_soc_pctbattery fuel gaugeneeds instrumentation

Calibration freezes what the board can sense (thermal is required) and records the rest as unsensed. Those edges honestly never earn, and verdicts use conservative per-class defaults for those factors — and say so. Thermal + memory alone already answer the core duty-cycle question.

Add a sensor later and the installation adapts. Wire the hardware, run the same --calibrate command again: frozen values never change — the new observable's band is frozen as a dated addendum, its edges start earning from their priors, and verdicts widen from default toward earned on that factor as evidence accumulates. Payloads adapt live with no calibration at all: POST /v0/payloads any time. Tell us what your carrier exposes and we'll map the readers.

API reference — v0

Host-local HTTP (loopback, port 8800 by default), JSON in and out, no auth by design — remote exposure, if any, is your OS's decision to mediate. Verdicts are microsecond-cheap graph arithmetic: re-ask freely; every verdict carries valid_for_s.

endpointpurpose
GET/v0/healthstatus, kernel version, frozen flag
GET/v0/envelopelive headroom per observable, regime, learning state
GET/v0/verdict/{payload_id}RUN / HOLD / STOP_BY for a registered payload's declared envelope
POST/v0/verdictprice a hypothetical run — the request-quote form (below)
GET/v0/payloadsthe registry
POST/v0/payloadsregister / update an envelope → 201
POST/v0/run/start · /v0/run/stopannounce transitions — commissioning context, not alarms
GET/v0/edgesthe causal graph as learned: Z and W per edge
GET/v0/signals?since_seq=Ntyped outbox + the byte ledger
GET/v0/receipts/{edge_id}the click-through: earned history behind a verdict
POST/v0/downlinksimulated ground pass — advances the outbox cursor, returns the pass receipt
POST/v0/bench/fault bench onlyinject the cooling-degradation fault the model doesn't know

Errors are structured: 404 unknown_payload includes the known list; malformed bodies get 400 invalid_json / invalid_request, never a 500.

The verdict object

Real output, bench mode (an earned thermal edge at Z = 0.97):

{
  "payload_id": "imager",
  "verdict": "RUN",                      // RUN | HOLD | STOP_BY
  "max_duration_s": 100961,
  "stop_by": "2026-09-01T05:51:15Z",
  "limiting_factor": "pack_soc_pct",
  "confidence": {
    "basis": "earned",                  // earned | default | mixed
    "min_Z": 0.971,
    "edges": ["payload_duty_cycle_th|soc_temp_c",
              "power_draw_soc_drawdown|pack_soc_pct"]
  },
  "because": "...",                      // plain-language reasoning
  "valid_for_s": 300,
  "receipts": ["/v0/receipts/payload_duty_cycle_th|soc_temp_c"]
}

A HOLD carries retry_after_s and the limiting factor. A running payload's verdict can become STOP_BY — advisory revocation, never a kill; honoring it is the integration contract. Where composing edges haven't earned, the verdict uses the default table for the payload's thermal_class and says so; as Z crosses the threshold, the same query silently upgrades.

The request-quote form

Describe the run you want; NM prices it against the live causal state. NM quotes — it never reserves; your scheduler keeps the calendar.

POST /v0/verdict
{
  "power_w": 6.0,          // or energy_wh
  "duration_s": 1800,
  "duty": 0.9,             // optional, default 1.0
  "mem_mb": 400,           // optional resource floor
  "finish_by": "2026-08-30T19:00:00Z",   // optional deadline
  "payload_id": "imager"   // optional — ad-hoc requests are legal
}

Answers: RUN (full window available now), HOLD with available_now_s (physics grants part — take the partial or retry), or a refusal that names the factor and why.

Python client

sdk/nm_advisor_client.py — one stdlib-only file; copy it next to your payload manager. As a library:

from nm_advisor_client import Advisor
nm = Advisor()                          # http://127.0.0.1:8800

v = nm.verdict("imager")
if v["verdict"] == "RUN":
    nm.run_start("imager")             # announce the start
    ...                                 # re-ask while running; honor STOP_BY
    nm.run_stop("imager")

q = nm.quote(power_w=6.0, duration_s=1800)
nm.register({"payload_id": "sar-01", "class": "compute", ...})
sig = nm.signals(since_seq=0)           # the typed outbox

As a CLI: health, envelope, edges, verdict <id>, quote --power-w 6 --duration-s 1800, start/stop <id>, signals, receipt <edge_id> — and manage <id>, a working reference manager loop (ask → start if RUN → re-ask → honor STOP_BY) that shows exactly what your scheduler does natively. API errors raise AdvisorError with the status and decoded body.

Payload envelopes

Each payload declares an envelope — in payloads.json, via POST /v0/payloads, or as an nm: block in your packaging manifest:

nm:
  payload_id: sar-imager-01
  class: compute          # compute | radio | actuation | housekeeping
  expected_power_w: 6.5   # steady-state draw at duty=1.0
  expected_duty: 0.8      # fraction of wall time active while running
  thermal_class: hot      # hot | warm | cool — drives conservative defaults
  min_run_s: 120          # shorter than this isn't worth starting
  preemptible: true       # can honor STOP_BY mid-run
  priority: 2             # tie-break hint; NM advises, never schedules

The declared wattage is what verdicts budget against; the learned edges refine how the board responds to that load, never what you claimed it draws.

Signals & the byte ledger

Nothing in the verdict path touches the network — the loop closes on the device. What crosses a link, when you choose to sync, is typed and kilobyte-class:

signalmeaningsize
anomalyacute surprise on a stable, earned edge — the one thing that alarms≤ ~1 KB
curiositychronic unknown — a hypothesis is wanted from the ground side≤ ~1 KB
promotean edge earned; carries full provenance~1 KB

The ledger accounts for every byte (GET /v0/signals returns it alongside the queue). Link usage scales with surprise, not runtime — a quiet week costs bytes. Announced transitions (run/start, run/stop) give the engine commissioning context so an expected thermal step is learned, not alarmed; only unannounced change alarms.

FAQ

Does it need the network?
Only at sync time, and only from your side. The device learns fully offline — the same posture it will have in orbit.
Can it break our system?
It holds no actuators. Worst case is a wrong suggestion — which its own certainty math then erodes. Every hardware read is defensive; a missing sensor means an omitted field, never a fake one.
Why does it refuse to deploy or verdict sometimes?
The gates are the product. No learning before the scale freezes; no earned basis before edges earn; a kernel that fails its self-check refuses to learn at all. When it refuses, it's telling you the number you'd have gotten wasn't real.
What's in the black box?
Less than you'd think. The learning law is ~25 published lines of math (papers at nervousmachine.com); it ships compiled and hash-verified. Everything you interact with — the config, the envelopes, the verdicts, the receipts — is inspectable text, and GET /v0/receipts/<edge> shows the evidence behind any number.
What does a wrong verdict cost?
Your scheduler keeps every decision, so a wrong suggestion costs one conservative hold or one early stop — and the error that caused it feeds back into the edge that made it.
