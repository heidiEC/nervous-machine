Launch autoresearch

What other industries already solved for launch

Agents propose techniques from aviation, chip cooling, nuclear power, semiconductors and more for launch's hardest problems. A causal harness keeps or discards each one against a rule written down before it runs, and remembers everything that was tried.

How it works

The graph behind it starts from every orbital launch attempt since 1957 and the published root causes of failures, then links in evidence from dozens of other fields.

Propose

A technique from somewhere else

An agent (or a person) names a technique proven in another field and the launch problem it should help. It reads what's already been tried first, so nothing is proposed twice.

Evaluate

Through the causal graph

The harness traces which launch outcomes the idea would move and how certain each link is. It flags conditions that rule it out (cryogenic, vacuum, vibration) and side effects it would bring in. Ideas only earn credit from evidence across several programs.

Keep or discard

Against a rule set in advance

A step is kept only if it improves the current set of practices by more than it costs to integrate. Every decision, and its reason, is recorded. Nothing here counts as proven in launch until launch evidence earns it.

What the launch record already says

From 7,186 orbital launch attempts (J. McDowell's GCAT catalog) and published failure investigations.

Maturity dominates failure

An engine's first flights carry about ten times the failure odds of a mature one, more than any choice of propellant or design. Propellant chemistry doesn't predict reliability once maturity is accounted for.

New rockets fail from design, mature ones from production

Since 2000, most failures on a vehicle's first three flights involved a new design element. Failures of mature vehicles are mostly manufacturing defects and contamination.

Every propellant trades one pollutant for another

Kerosene leaves soot high in the atmosphere. Solid motors leave alumina and chlorine in the stratosphere. Hydrogen is cleaner but usually flies with solid boosters.

The evidence for reuse is one program deep

Reuse cuts new hardware per tonne sharply, but nearly all of that evidence comes from a single vehicle family. The harness won't give reuse full credit until a second program confirms it.

Become a contributor

Contributors bring ideas, evidence, or agent compute. Proposals run through the same harness, and you see how each one fared.

We review every request. Contributions stay private until reviewed, and the graph itself isn't published.

Please don't send export-controlled technical data.

Propose published techniques and say where they work. Leave out detailed designs, drawings or performance data for specific vehicles or engines. We may ask where you're based and who you work with, because launch technology is subject to export controls.
